PROJECT
A 10.410-second cinematic documentary motion graphic:
1. A blue dimensional bar chart builds from 2009 to 2018.
2. Its final bar turns gold and a gold growth line connects the values.
3. A large Jet Airways aircraft appears over the chart.
4. The chart dissolves into a dark aviation scene with moving clouds and smaller passenger aircraft passing behind the main aircraft.

ANIMATION TIMELINE
Use these as reconstruction targets, adjusting slightly if the original reference is available.

0.00–3.85 seconds — sequential chart construction:
• Begin with the navy background and axes already visible.
• The first bar is beginning to rise at the opening; do not add a long intro.
• Reveal bars from 2009 through 2018 in chronological order.
• Start a new bar approximately every 0.38 seconds.
• Each bar grows vertically from the baseline over approximately 0.40 seconds.
• Use smooth cubic ease-out, with no spring, bounce, or overshoot.
• Keep the extrusion coherent as each bar grows.
• Reveal each year with its corresponding bar.
• Fade in each final value label as the bar approaches its finished height.
• Labels show the final values; do not animate numerical counters.
• Previously completed bars remain visible.

Framing during the build:
• Start with a slightly closer view of the first few bars.
• Gradually pull back and reframe so the complete ten-bar chart fits comfortably.
• Keep this subtle and continuous, approximately a 1.2× to 1.0× scale adjustment.
• Animate the graph and axes as one coordinated scene.
• Settle the framing by approximately 3.85 seconds.

3.75–4.30 seconds — completed growth chart:
• Finish the 2018 bar and change it to gold.
• Draw a thin gold line from left to right across the tops of the bars.
• The line follows the actual values, including the small dip from 2011 to 2012.
• Add small restrained gold markers at the data points.
• Keep the line around 2–3 px thick at internal-stage resolution.
• Let the finished graph register briefly before the transition.
• No dramatic flash or camera shake.

4.30–5.35 seconds — transition into aviation:
• Introduce a realistic Jet Airways passenger aircraft in front of the chart.
• Aircraft faces left: nose left, tail right, visible side and slight underside.
• White fuselage, dark navy tail, gold Jet Airways emblem and gold/navy detailing.
• Preserve the realistic wing, engine, and landing-gear details of the selected source image.
• Bring it in through a short smooth opacity/position transition, settling near the centre.
• Main aircraft occupies approximately 40–43% of the internal-stage width.
• Position its centre around (960, 470), slightly above the stage’s vertical centre.
• The chart remains briefly visible behind the aircraft, then fades away as a complete group.
• Simultaneously fade in clouds across the bottom and smaller aircraft at different depths.
• By around 5.35 seconds, chart bars, axes, values, years, and gold line have fully disappeared.
• This is an overlapping dissolve, not a hard cut or a morph of bars into aircraft.

5.35–10.410 seconds — layered aircraft scene:
• Keep the large Jet Airways aircraft as the visual anchor near the centre.
• It faces left and remains mostly steady, with only very subtle positional drift.
• Do not rotate it dramatically or animate its wings.
• Place smaller realistic white passenger aircraft with orange/red tails behind it.
• Most smaller aircraft face and travel toward the right.
• Use several horizontal flight lanes:
  – upper sky around y=240–320;
  – upper-middle around y=380–450;
  – lower-middle around y=660–780;
  – low background around y=850–930.
• Stagger their starts and let them enter and exit beyond the stage edges.
• Show around 3–6 secondary aircraft at any given time.
• Vary their widths approximately between 140 and 350 px to create depth.
• Distant aircraft: smaller, dimmer, softer, slower.
• Nearer secondary aircraft: somewhat larger and faster, while remaining behind the hero aircraft.
• Use smooth horizontal movement with no random wobbling.
• Add subtle horizontal motion trails behind the passing aircraft.
• Trails should be low-opacity grey-blue streaks, not thick white contrails or neon beams.
• Keep the hero aircraft sharper than the moving background aircraft.

Clouds:
• Photographic-looking dark blue-grey clouds fill the lower quarter to third of the internal stage.
• Use multiple soft layers with slow horizontal parallax.
• Include small illuminated patches within the clouds, maintaining the dark overall exposure.
• Clouds should not obscure the hero aircraft.
• No cartoon cloud shapes, hard-edged smoke, or obvious repeating texture seams.

Ending:
• Continue the aircraft and cloud movement until the final frame.
• Do not add an outro, logo, fade to black, or sudden zoom.
• Preserve the smaller centred presentation through the ending.
